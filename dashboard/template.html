Wind SCADA performance review · public data · 2014 to 2015

La Haute Borne wind farm performance

Four Senvion MM82 turbines (2.05 MW each, 80 m hub, 82 m rotor) in north-eastern France. This review turns two years of 10-minute SCADA data into power curves, lost production by cause, recurring derates and a long-term corrected P50 and P90 energy estimate. Use the filters to look at any turbine, period or derate threshold.

Data quality

How it was calculated

Data checks. Timestamps are converted to UTC and duplicate records around daylight saving changes are removed. Intervals with missing or implausible power or wind speed, or a wind speed frozen for an hour or more, are excluded. Implausible pitch and temperature values are blanked.

Power curves. Wind speed is normalised to the site mean air density (ERA5 density at 100 m) with the cube-root rule used for pitch-regulated turbines. Each turbine's reference curve is the median power in 0.5 m/s bins, built only from normal operation: producing, no plant availability or curtailment loss, normal pitch for the wind speed, and within a robust band of the bin median.

Lost production. Every 10-minute interval is classed as downtime, curtailment, derate, suspected icing, underperformance or normal. Lost energy is expected power minus actual power, counted only for the five loss classes. A derate means pitch above the turbine's normal pitch for that wind speed by more than the selected threshold, with power below 90 percent of expected.

Derate events. Consecutive derate intervals on one turbine form an event (gaps up to 20 minutes are bridged; events under 30 minutes are dropped). Recurrence is measured by hour of day and outdoor temperature to separate operational patterns from random faults.

Long-term energy. Monthly revenue meter energy, grossed up for reported availability and curtailment losses, is regressed on monthly ERA5 and MERRA-2 wind speed and applied to 20 years of reanalysis. A 10,000 draw Monte Carlo varies the reanalysis product, meter and loss errors, the regression (bootstrapped months) and the long-term years. Net AEP subtracts measured availability and curtailment losses.

Cross-checks. The P50 is compared with NREL's open-source OpenOA reference implementation on the same data, and the estimated downtime loss is compared with the availability loss the plant itself reported.

Limits. There is no warranted power curve in the open data, so turbines are compared with their own normal operation. Two years of operation limit the long-term regression. Nacelle wind speed is affected by the rotor, so power curves here are for monitoring and loss estimation, not for warranty testing.
